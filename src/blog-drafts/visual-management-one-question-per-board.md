Visual management is easy to start and easy to overdo. A wall of charts nobody reads is worse than one board everyone uses. A simple test helps: each board, mark or signal should answer one question, for anyone walking past, in a few seconds.

![Visual management: one question per board. Six questions, the tools that answer them, and an electronics manufacturing example for each](/blog-images/visual-management.png)

## Six questions worth answering at a glance

1. **Where does it go?** Shadow boards, floor marking and labelled locations give everything a home, so missing items stand out. Feeder carts, stencils, squeegees and test fixtures are good places to start on an electronics line.
2. **What's happening now?** Andon lights and line status boards show at once when a station has stopped or needs help, so the right person goes straight there.
3. **Are we on track?** An hour-by-hour board shows plan against actual while there's still time to recover. Write the reason for any shortfall next to the number, in the operator's words.
4. **What's the right way?** Standard work and one-point lessons show the method at the point of use: one photo-led sheet for a reflow profile set-up beats a binder in the office.
5. **What's next?** Kanban cards and boards signal what to make or move next, such as an empty bin card that triggers the next kit to be picked.
6. **What needs attention?** Red tags and problem boards make abnormal things visible and give each one an owner, such as moisture-sensitive parts past their floor life.

## Making it stick

- **Design it with the people who use it.** If the team can't read the board in five seconds, change the board, not the team.
- **Keep it current.** A board updated at the start of every shift is trusted; one updated weekly is ignored. Make updating it part of the job.
- **Act on what it shows.** A short stand-up at the board, with problems given an owner and a date, is what turns a display into a tool.
- **Use colour consistently.** Green for normal, amber for at risk, red for stopped, the same everywhere on site.
- **Retire what nobody uses.** Walk the floor every few months and take down anything that no longer answers a question.
