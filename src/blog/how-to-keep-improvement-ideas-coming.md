The best improvement ideas usually come from the people doing the job. They see the extra walk to the stores, the fixture that never quite fits and the check that catches nothing. Keeping those ideas coming is mostly about what happens in the first few days after someone speaks up.

![How to keep improvement ideas coming: six steps from an idea being raised to the person being thanked by name](/blog-images/improvement-ideas.png)

## Six habits that help

1. **Make it easy to raise.** One line on a card or a board is enough to start. The detail can come later, once someone has had a look.
2. **Reply within 48 hours.** Every idea gets an answer, even if it's "not yet, and here's why". A quick reply tells people their idea was read.
3. **Let the team try it small.** Agree limits up front for cost, safety and quality. Within those limits, people can test a change without waiting for sign-off.
4. **Measure it.** A simple before-and-after, such as cycle time, defects or walking distance, shows everyone what changed.
5. **Make it the standard.** Update the work instruction and the training, so the gain sticks when people change shifts or roles.
6. **Thank the person by name, in front of the team.** It costs nothing, and it's the clearest sign that ideas are wanted.

When people see their idea used, they bring the next one.

## Putting it into practice

Start with one area and one board. Agree who replies and by when, and what the team can change without asking. Review the board at the start of a regular meeting, so ideas never wait more than a few days.
