Gemba, kaizen, 5S, kanban and poka-yoke often get used as if they all meant "doing lean". They don't. Each one does a different job, so it helps to start from the problem in front of you and pick the tool that fits.

![Which lean tool for which problem? Five problems, the lean tool for each, and an electronics manufacturing example](/blog-images/lean-tools.png)

## Gemba: when you're guessing at the cause

Gemba means the real place, where the work happens. Go there, watch and ask questions before deciding anything. Twenty minutes at the rework bench before the first-pass yield meeting will often tell you more than the report on the table.

## Kaizen: when the job is harder than it needs to be

Kaizen means small improvements, made often, mostly by the people doing the job. A hand-solder operator moving the tip cleaner closer to their working hand won't make a headline, but dozens of changes like it add up. Our post on [keeping improvement ideas coming](/blog/how-to-keep-improvement-ideas-coming/) covers how to keep them flowing.

## 5S: when time goes on looking for things

5S gives everything a home and keeps it there: Sort, Set in order, Shine, Standardise, Sustain. A labelled stencil rack shows at a glance which stencil is out. The last S is the hard one, so build the checks into the shift rather than holding a one-off clear-out.

## Kanban: when you have too much of one part and too little of another

With kanban, you replace only what has been used. Two bins of connectors at a box build station is the simple version: when one bin empties, it goes back to stores to be refilled while the station works from the other. It suits parts you use regularly, such as consumables, common hardware and repeat-build items. Customer-specific parts on low-volume jobs are usually better kitted to the job.

## Poka-yoke: when the same mistake keeps coming back

Poka-yoke means mistake-proofing: designing the step so the error is hard to make, or is caught the moment it happens. A barcode check that stops the wrong reel going on a feeder is one example; a test fixture that only takes the assembly one way is another. If the fix for a repeat defect is "remind the operators", it's worth asking what poka-yoke would look like instead.

## How they work together

The five fit together. Gemba shows you the problem. 5S makes problems easier to see. Kanban steadies the flow of parts. Kaizen gives everyone a way to fix what they find, and poka-yoke stops the worst problems coming back.
